Focus

Assess and adjust: analyze your Cycle 1 evidence and adjust your coaching live in response to the profiles and qualities of the players on site.

Between IPM 1 and IPM 2, you coach the players in the team at your club and complete A4 micro-cycle 1. Identify a pattern within a principle, or connected principles, of the game model. The pattern can come out of micro-cycle 1, but it does not have to. Arrive at IPM 2 with evidence of the pattern and a session designed to meet the players’ needs. At the IPM you coach players you do not know, so you profile them first and adjust the plan to fit them.

The Identified Game Pattern (IGP)

An IGP is a consistent way players respond to challenges or opportunities within a principle of the game model, which the coach has seen across several sessions and games and wants to develop. The pattern sits inside a principle, so the principle sets the wider frame for the session, including how the players on site express it.

Example

In build up, the center backs play forward to progress against an opponent who presses, but against a compact block they pass sideways when options to play forward are there or could be provoked.

Choose one IGP

Draw the pattern from the periodization plan in A3 and/or A4 micro-cycle 1, then observe it in implementation and analyze it through qualitative and quantitative indicators. The pattern can go either way:

A
Behind the plan

The pattern is challenging and player behavior is behind where the plan expects it to be.

B
On track

Player behavior is on track and the next phase adds progression, variation, complexity or individualization.

What You Bring to IPM 2

Clips

Four to six clips of the players in the team that show different expressions of the pattern, each with a timestamp.

Measures

  • The instance and its components
  • The count across sessions and games
  • Individual measures for the key players
One Session
  • An activation
  • A small number activity, 5v5 or smaller
  • A large number activity, 8v8 or larger
  • Any extra activities you want

For each activity, plan three things, all within the number constraint above:

More challenging

A harder version of the activity.

Less challenging

An easier version of the activity.

Adjustments

The ones you expect you may need to make.

After you observe the players before or at the IPM to profile their qualities, you choose which version of each activity to start with. You will not cover every situation. The plan shows how you intend to respond to players.

During the profiling period

Identify each player’s qualities, what they need next and how that aligns with what you planned. Then adjust the session plan:

  • Which players and which needs the activities serve
  • Which version of each activity to start with: more or less challenging
  • Where, when and how to individualize

Assessment

IPM 2 is not assessed. The Coach Educator assesses the coach’s work through the A4 micro-cycle submissions and the individual meeting after each one. Micro-cycle 2 is formative and micro-cycle 3 is summative.

For Coach Educators

Coach Educator Preparation Guide

Each coach arrives at IPM 2 with a pattern from the players in the team at their club and a session planned to work on it. At the IPM the coach works with players they do not know. Your work is to support each coach to profile those players, adjust the plan to them and reflect on how well the session met their needs.

1
Between IPM 1 and IPM 2
Assess

Review each coach’s IGP, clips and measures, and plan IPM 2 with that coach.

2
Before the IPM
Plan

Check the session plan and its versions, and confirm the site setup.

3
Before they coach
Adjust

Ask whether anything needs consideration or adjustment.

4
At the IPM
Implement

Observe the session against the adjusted plan, with the coach’s A1 learning focus and your own observation of their needs in mind.

5
After they coach
Reflect

Facilitate and support the reflection on how well the session met player needs.

What to check in each coach’s IGP

The IGP is drawn from the periodization plan and analyzed through qualitative and quantitative indicators. The coach states which way it goes: behind the plan, or on track with a next phase that adds progression, variation, complexity or individualization.

Before the IPM: site setup, video and rosters

Confirm your site’s setup with the operations team. Coaches profile players in a different way for each setup (Optimal, Ideal or Workable, in the profiling table above). When video and rosters arrive, the operations team sends them to the coaches so they can profile the key players in each pattern before IPM 2. The video and rosters include:

  • Roster by number, position and name
  • Video of the players coaches will work with
  • Which night each team plays

Preparing for each coach’s pattern

  1. 01

    Key players involved in the IGP

  2. 02

    What the coach plans to observe in each player

  3. 03

    Parts of the session plan that still align with their home club context, and which do not

  4. 04

    How the coach plans to adjust based on observations

Bring to your individual plan

How will the coach decide what to change once they observe players they have not met?

Individualizing IPM 2 for each coach

Plan IPM 2 with each coach from what you observed at IPM 1 and in A4 micro-cycle 1.

From IPM 1 and micro-cycle 1, identify

  • What the coach does well
  • One or two areas for the coach to develop
  • How the coach learns and responds to feedback

Your plan for this coach

  • The moments at IPM 2 you are most focused on
  • Learning opportunities: questions, peer observation, a second attempt if available
  • How you and the coach will both identify that the coach developed

In the classroom: the pattern presentations

Ask the questions to test how the pattern, the clips and the session connect, and how ready the coach is to adjust for new players.

Listen for

  • The pattern’s place in the periodization plan
  • How the clips show each instance of the pattern, through its components
  • What the session is meant to do, and for which players

Questions to open with

  • How does the pattern connect to the players’ qualities, needs and the game model?
  • Where does the data support what is seen in the clips, and where does it not capture it?
  • Who are the key players, what is the plan to influence them, and how does the session objective connect to as many other players in the team as possible?

Before they coach: profiling and adjusting

Support the coach to profile the key players in the pattern first, then the players closest to them, then the players farthest away.

Ask about players

  • Each player’s qualities
  • How each player engages
  • What each player needs next

Ask about the plan

  • Which players the activities serve
  • The starting version of each activity
  • Where the coach individualizes
  • Which observation led to each change
Before the coach steps on the field

The adjusted plan: changes marked, each with a reason.

Version chosen: more or less challenging, and why.

During the session

Adjustments: observed during play.

The coach planned the session for the players in the team at their club. Ask how each change fits the players available and the pattern.

At the IPM: observing the session

The players are new to the coach, so observe the session against the profiles and the adjusted plan.

Look for

  • Whether the starting version fit the players the coach profiled
  • When the coach changed versions, and what the players showed
  • Whether the coach individualized for the key players in the pattern

What to watch for at IPM 2

  • The coach implements the plan built for the players at their club and does not adjust to the player qualities in the session
  • The profiles do not change the starting version
  • The influence on the players when the coach adjusts for the group

After they coach: facilitating the reflection

Ask the coach

  • Meeting player needs. How well did the session meet the needs of the players available?
  • Adjustments. What did you adjust in the moment, and why?
  • Influence. How did you support players, what influence did you have and how do you know?

Your feedback

  • Compare the session with the individual plan you made with the coach
  • Compare the session with the plan the coach designed
  • Agree on the takeaways the coach will apply in the development period after IPM 2
  • Connect the takeaways to A4 micro-cycle 2
