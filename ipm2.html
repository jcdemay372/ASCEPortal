Plan, Adjust, Reflect  ·  College May 17–20  ·  Pro Jun 7–10, 2027

Focus

IPM 2 is Plan, Adjust, Reflect to Meet Player Needs. You arrive with a pattern from the players in the team and a session planned to work on it. At the IPM you coach players you do not know, so you profile them first and adjust the plan to fit them.

1
Between IPM 1 and IPM 2
Plan

Choose one Identified Game Pattern (IGP), gather the clips and measures, and plan a session to work on it.

2
At the IPM
Adjust

Profile the players you will coach and adjust the session plan to fit them.

3
After you coach
Reflect

Review how well the session met the needs of the players available.

Between IPM 1 and IPM 2

Work with the players in the team and look for patterns of development and performance, for individual players and for the team. Choose one Identified Game Pattern (IGP) inside a principle or principles of the game model, and place it in the periodization plan from A3. The pattern can go either way:

A
Behind the plan

The pattern is challenging and is behind where the plan expects it to be.

B
On track

The pattern is on track, and the next phase adds progression, variation, complexity or individualization.

Example

In build up, the center backs play forward to progress against an opponent who presses, but against a compact block they pass sideways when options to play forward are there or could be provoked.

Clips

Four to six clips of the players in the team that show different expressions of the pattern, each with a timestamp.

Measures

  • The instance and its components
  • The count across sessions and games
  • The individual measures for the focus players
One Session in the IPM 2 Session Planner
  • An activation
  • A small activity, 5v5 or smaller
  • A large game, with the maximum players available
  • Any extra activities you want

For each activity, plan the design (non game form, modified game form or game form), the practice approach (directed, guided or invited) and the coaching interactions that go with that pairing.

For each activity, also plan a more challenging version, a less challenging version, and the adjustments you expect to make, written as “if I observe …, I will …”. You will not cover every situation. The point is to show how you plan to respond to players.

In the Classroom

You present the pattern to the other coaches in the cohort:

  1. 01

    What you observed in training and games

  2. 02

    The clips and measures that show it

  3. 03

    Which phase of the periodization plan it belongs to

  4. 04

    What the session is meant to do for the players

The Other Coaches and Your Coach Educator Ask

How does the pattern connect to the players’ qualities and the game model?

Before you coach, you observe the players you will work with. How that works depends on the site:

During the profiling period

Identify each player’s qualities and what they need next. Then adjust the session plan:

  • Which players the activities serve
  • Which version of each activity to start with
  • Where to individualize

After You Coach

Reflect with your Coach Educator and the other coaches on how well the session met the needs of the players available. Identify what you adjusted in the moment and why, and the takeaways you will apply with the players in the team in A4 micro-cycle 2.

The Exercise Tests Three Things Together
  • How well you understand the game
  • How you profile players
  • How you apply your understanding of human development when the players are new to you

Assessment

IPM 2 is not assessed. The Coach Educator assesses the coach’s work through the A4 micro-cycle submissions and the individual meeting after each one. Micro-cycle 2 is formative and micro-cycle 3 is summative.

For Coach Educators

Coach Educator Guide

Each coach arrives at IPM 2 with an Identified Game Pattern (IGP) from the players in the team, clips and measures that show it, and a planned session. Your work is to help each coach observe the players available, adjust the plan to them, and reflect on how well the session met their needs. The four objectives run in order: observe, plan, adjust, reflect.

Before the IPM

Confirm the setup for your site with the operations team: the same team all week or a different team each night, and whether video and rosters go out two weeks ahead. When the site has video and rosters, send them to the coaches the day you receive them.

Review each coach’s material before arrival. Start from their A4 micro-cycle 1 work and check four things:

  1. 01

    The pattern is part of a principle in the game model, and the coach states which phase of the periodization plan it belongs to.

  2. 02

    The coach states whether the pattern is behind the plan or on track, and what the next step is: progression, variation, complexity or individualization.

  3. 03

    The clips show different expressions of the pattern, and the measures define the instance and its components.

  4. 04

    The session plan has more and less challenging versions and planned adjustments for each activity.

Identify one or two areas to address with each coach and point a direction. Leave the solution to the coach.

In the classroom

Each coach presents the pattern to the other coaches. Open the questions to the cohort first, and keep the players at the center: what the players need, and how the game model supports them in meeting the pattern. At the IPM the coaches in the cohort work as staff for each other, so each coach shows staff collaboration through how they work with the other coaches.

The profiling period

Observation comes before influence. During the 20-minute game, or the video review where the site has it, ask each coach what they observe about individual players: their qualities, how they engage, and what they need next. Before the coach steps on the field, ask what changed in the plan and which observations drove the change.

Observing the session

Start from the adjusted plan, since you need to know the design to make sense of what you see. Look for:

  • Which design and practice approach the coach chose for each activity, and whether the choice came from what the players need
  • Whether the interactions align with that design, and whether the coach observed before reinforcing or correcting
  • How often the pattern reached each player, and whether the coach moved to the more or less challenging version when the players showed it was needed

The reflection

Ask how well the session met the needs of the players available, what the coach adjusted in the moment and why, and what the coach will take back to the players in the team for A4 micro-cycle 2. Each gap is a conversation about what the players needed and what the design was for.
