Assessment and Development

How assessment works in the A-Senior Course

Assessment is an ongoing process of collecting, interpreting and using evidence of student learning and development. It supports students in understanding their progress, reflecting on their experiences, identifying areas for development and adapting their practice in relation to established competencies and standards.

This page brings together the Assessment Philosophy, Learning & Development Framework and the A-Senior Course Guide. The rubrics for each assignment are on the Rubrics page.

As, for and of learning

Assessment as learning

You interpret evidence and reflect on your practice.

Students examine their coaching practice, interpret evidence, reflect on their experiences and make informed decisions about their continued learning and development.

Assessment for learning

You and Coach Educators use feedback and dialogue to identify next steps.

Students and Coach Educators use observations, evidence, professional dialogue and feedback to understand progress, identify development priorities and support continued learning.

Assessment of learning

Accumulated evidence informs the course completion decision.

Accumulated evidence of coaching practice is interpreted in relation to the Course Learning Outcome and competency outcomes to determine whether students have demonstrated the expected course standards.

These are interconnected purposes within one learning journey. Evidence is considered holistically, not as isolated scores or a count of completed tasks.

The micro-cycle ruleAcross the three Applied Coaching micro-cycles, the standard must be met in two of the three, with micro-cycle 3 among them. A micro-cycle meets the standard when there is sufficient evidence of competency capability across that micro-cycle, and no one strength in a micro-cycle makes up for another.

Each coach's context is different, and the course standard stays the same.

Four assessment principles

  1. 01

    All four assignments are assessed together as one body of evidence.

  2. 02

    Sufficient evidence is required for all nine competency outcomes. A strength in one does not make up for another.

  3. 03

    Participation or submission alone does not demonstrate competence. Unsafe coaching practice cannot meet the expected standards.

  4. 04

    The standard must be met in two of the three micro-cycles, with micro-cycle 3 among them.

Evidence and Dialogue

Professional dialogue and evidence

Professional dialogue is an integral part of assessment. Through purposeful questioning, feedback and reflection, students and Coach Educators develop a shared understanding of coaching practice, progress and priorities for continued development.

Evidence may come from course assignments, observations of authentic coaching practice, coaching plans and other relevant resources, student reflections, professional dialogue and feedback. Evidence should be relevant, authentic and sufficient to support an informed understanding of student development in relation to the Course Learning Outcome and competency outcomes.

Coach Educators facilitate this process through observation, questioning, dialogue and feedback. They also have responsibility for interpreting evidence in relation to the expected course standards and supporting fair and consistent assessment decisions.

Documentation should be purposeful and proportionate. The emphasis is on the quality and relevance of evidence rather than the quantity of materials submitted.

Holistic assessment does not mean that strengths in one competency compensate for an expected outcome that has not been demonstrated in another. Sufficient evidence is required for all nine outcomes, but a single authentic coaching experience may demonstrate several outcomes together.

Leadership & Professional Development is integrated throughout coaching practice and is assessed through the nine competency outcomes. Leadership & Professional Development, safeguarding, inclusion, player wellbeing and ethical responsibilities are not optional and cannot be offset by strengths elsewhere.

Safe, ethical, and player-centered coaching practice is fundamental to the expected standards. Unsafe coaching practice cannot meet those standards.
Course Completion

Completion decisions

Assessment decisions are based on accumulated evidence of your coaching practice in relation to the A-Senior Course Learning Outcome and all nine expected competency outcomes.

Assessment decision

Satisfactory

You have demonstrated sufficient capability in relation to all expected competency outcomes and met the course completion requirements.

Assessment decision

Provisional

You have not yet demonstrated sufficient capability in relation to one or more expected competency outcomes, but the identified development needs can reasonably be addressed through additional learning and assessment within the permitted period.

Assessment decision

Unsatisfactory

You have not demonstrated sufficient capability in relation to the expected competency outcomes, and the identified gaps cannot reasonably be addressed within the permitted additional development period, or the additional development requirements have not been met.

A Provisional decision provides an opportunity for additional development; it is not a final successful completion outcome.

Additional development and reassessment

If you receive a Provisional decision, you may be granted an additional development period of up to 16 weeks to address identified development needs and demonstrate sufficient capability in relation to the outstanding competency outcomes. This period provides opportunities to continue learning, apply feedback, adapt your coaching practice and generate further evidence through authentic coaching experiences.

An individualized development plan will identify the outstanding competency outcomes, development priorities, expected evidence, available support, and timeline for reassessment. The expected A-Senior Course standards remain unchanged. At the conclusion of the additional development period, your accumulated evidence, including any new evidence, will be reassessed against the outstanding competency outcomes to determine your final completion decision. The period begins when the Provisional decision and development requirements are communicated to you in writing.

Course Completion and Development Report

Your Course Completion and Development Report provides an evidence-informed summary of your learning and development. Your report will be discussed with you through professional dialogue, helping you recognize your progress and identify meaningful opportunities for continued development.

Students who successfully complete the U.S. Soccer A-Senior Course will be awarded a U.S. Soccer A Diploma and an A License valid for three years. The Diploma does not expire; the License must be renewed every three years in accordance with U.S. Soccer requirements.

Consistency

Calibration, moderation and review

Assessment consistency is supported through shared expectations, Coach Educator preparation, professional dialogue, calibration and moderation of assessment decisions. Moderation provides opportunities to review the interpretation of evidence, discuss assessment decisions and strengthen consistency across courses and delivery contexts.

The Course Educator Lead is responsible for ensuring that assessment procedures are followed, decisions are appropriately documented and any concerns or disagreements are addressed through established U.S. Soccer procedures.

Students must receive clear information about assessment expectations, course completion decisions and their right to request a review or submit an appeal. The U.S. Soccer assessment review and appeals procedure is available in the Learning Center.
