Course Purpose

Leading development and performance across a senior season

The U.S. Soccer A Senior Course prepares coaches to lead player and team development and performance across a senior competitive season. The course is intended for coaches working with senior players in an 11v11 environment, with responsibility for leading player and team development and performance through a game model and a systematic coaching approach across a competitive season.

The Player

Design a player development process that applies across your whole roster; integrate individual characteristics, readiness and performance demands; and systematically monitor player development and progress to inform individual and team decisions.

The Game

Construct and implement a game model across all moments of the game that reflects your playing identity, your players and your competitive environment; evaluate player, team and opposition performance; and adapt game plans while balancing development and performance objectives.

The Environment

Periodize a season and individualize it through a stated method; design training and game experiences connected to your game model; coach in the moment and carry what happens in the game into the following week of training.

Leadership & Professional Development

Examine what you believe about how players learn and develop and connect it to your practice; strengthen relationships and collaboration with players and multidisciplinary staff; track your own competency development; and take responsibility for your ongoing professional learning and leadership.

These areas develop together through one discipline: identify, respond, measure. Identify the behaviors your players need, respond through a teaching plan and coaching interactions, and measure your influence on their development and performance. The three in-person meetings apply it at increasing scale: a single coaching moment, then a cycle, then a whole season.

Progression from B to A. Building on the B Course, the A Senior Course extends your coaching practice from leading development across connected periodized cycles to leading player and team development and performance across a competitive season. You will connect and adapt development processes over time, balancing long-term player development and competitive demands.
How Coaches Learn

Four educational principles

The way we educate coaches should reflect the way we expect coaches to support players.

PrincipleWhat it means for your learning
HolisticConnect knowledge, decisions, behaviors, relationships and reflection in your coaching practice.
Reality-basedLearn through authentic coaching contexts, player needs and the realities of the game.
ExperientialApply, observe, receive feedback, reflect and adapt over time.
Autonomy-supportiveMake informed choices and take increasing ownership of your learning and development.
Self-regulated learning is central to this experience, as it is for the players in the team: You are expected to take increasing ownership of your learning, recognizing what you need, acting on feedback and adapting your development with the support of Coach Educators.
