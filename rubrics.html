The Standard

Rubrics

The A Senior Course assesses the nine competencies together, through the coaching practice in which they appear. The standard is the Course Learning Outcome and the nine competency expectations at A Senior level in the Course Guide. The rubrics support consistent assessment against that standard and add no standard of their own.

Each assignment brief sets out the work through which the coach shows the competencies, and what a complete submission includes. Each rubric describes what the work as a whole shows about the coach. The levels differ in how the coach connects the work to the players on the roster and across the assignments. There are no individual competency scores, and no single part of a submission decides the level. Each rubric lists the nine competencies, with the competencies connected in its assignment brief in bold, and the Coach Educator assesses them together through the work as a whole. In A4, Coaching Approach is the dominant and most observable competency.

The three levels

All the assignments connect as part of assessment as learning. When the Coach Educator assesses each assignment, the level shows progress toward the standard and provides evidence for the course completion decision. The course completion decision is made once all the work has been submitted and assessed.

Considering evidence

The Coach Educator considers the evidence holistically against the Course Learning Outcome and the nine competency expectations at A Senior level. The questions for the review support consistent evaluation of the evidence across the submission. The Student Coach and Coach Educator meet virtually to discuss the submission, ask clarifying questions, identify the coach's needs, and set development targets to carry forward in the course.

Across the course

Foundational and formative

A1, A2 and A3

They establish the foundations for applied coaching, and the Coach Educator helps the coach connect each assignment to the next.

Formative

A4 micro-cycles 1 and 2

The Coach Educator guides the coach on what to work on in the next micro-cycle.

Summative

A4 micro-cycle 3

It is the culminating opportunity in the course guide, and the Coach Educator assesses it against the evidence from the whole season.

Safeguarding. Unsafe coaching practice cannot meet the expected standards. When the coach creates, permits, or fails to address an unsafe physical or emotional playing environment, the work does not meet the standard, whatever the quality elsewhere.
By Assignment

Assignment rubrics and questions for the review

Across the three Applied Coaching micro-cycles, the standard must be met in two of the three with micro-cycle 3 among them. A micro-cycle meets the standard when the work across the whole micro-cycle shows sufficient evidence of competency capability.
